## Findings that matter for the Federations list and the discipline model

1. **There is no single distance per discipline.** IPSC fixes no shooting distance for handgun, shotgun or PCC. A course of fire is built per stage, with limits on round count and on the number of hits from one location, not on distance. The only handgun distance rules are safety minimums (7 m from steel). USPSA, IDPA and ICORE also set distances per stage. Only Steel Challenge has stage-specific plate distances, and those sit in 8 official stage diagrams. The current `discipline` record (one record per discipline and distance, `distance` required) cannot hold IPSC handgun, USPSA or ICORE courses as they are.
2. **The unit of competition is the stage, not the discipline.** In IPSC the course of fire is a separately timed and scored challenge, a stage holds one course, a match has at least 3 stages. Scores are summed over stages. A record for an ordinary target discipline (fixed distance, fixed shots, fixed face) does not map onto this.
3. **Scoring is not ring scoring.** IPSC and USPSA use zones A, C and D (A 5 points; C 4 Major or 3 Minor; D 2 Major or 1 Minor), plus a hit factor (points divided by time). IDPA and ICORE are "time plus": zones add seconds to the time. Steel Challenge scores time only, with 3 s per missed plate. There is no ring, decimal or inner ten.
4. **Divisions are equipment classes with a power factor, not distance classes.** Power factor is bullet weight in grains times velocity in feet per second. Minor floor is 125 in IPSC and USPSA. Major floor is 160 (IPSC Open), 170 (IPSC Standard, Classic, Revolver) and 165 (USPSA Open, Limited, Revolver). A shooter's score depends on the declared division, which is not a property of the stage.
5. **Country coverage for IPSC is large and regional.** IPSC's own regions page lists 111 regions (my count of its entries) in four zones. USPSA is the US region. A "Federations" list that wants countries must decide between listing IPSC once as an international body, or one record per region (I did not check regional sites for rule books of their own; the IPSC rule books page lists translations).
6. **Action Air is a discipline of IPSC, not a division and not a micro-region.** It has its own rule book (January 2026) with its own divisions, 6 mm projectiles and a maximum of 2 joules.
7. **Target shape and zone sizes can be cited, but the shapes are trademarked.** The IPSC Target is 45 cm wide and 57 cm high with zones A, C and D (read from IPSC's published diagram). IPSC registers "IPSC Targets" as marks. Shipping the outline in a public repository needs the owner's decision.
8. **The Steel Challenge and 3-Gun rule books are USPSA publications.** SCSA and Multigun are hosted and copyrighted by USPSA. They are separate rule books with their own divisions and scoring.

## IPSC (International Practical Shooting Confederation)

- Site: https://www.ipsc.org/ , rule books page: https://www.ipsc.org/ipsc-rules/rule-books/ (page states "Updated: December 31, 2025").
- Handgun rules, January 2026 edition, English PDF, linked from that page under "Handgun" (SHA-256 of the file read: `1ba68a56d5d84158c38a0bad9d0935f1ac035e8a4dd3aaaa2301f66576884668`, 81 pages). The PDF cover gives the publisher address in Mallorca, Spain and a rules e-mail address.
- The same page lists English books for .22LR handgun, shotgun, rifle, mini rifle, pistol caliber carbine, Action Air handgun, Action Air PCC, Action Air rifle, Grand Tournament, combined 3 and 6 discipline books and an Action Air combined book, plus interpretation notes (October 2025, February 2026, May 2026). Translations listed: Finnish, Portuguese (Brazil), Spanish, Serbian, Chinese, French (not all for every book).
- Free to read: yes. Licence: the cover states copyright by IPSC and reserves the acronyms IPSC, DVC and IROA, the shield logo, the IROA name, "IPSC Targets" and the motto "Diligentia, Vis, Celeritas" as registered marks, and bars their use by unaffiliated parties without written approval. The trademark notice (https://www.ipsc.org/trademark/) bars commercial use, reproduction, modification and display of its marks without written consent. No reuse licence for the text.
- History: the 1976 Columbia Conference page (https://www.ipsc.org/1976-columbia-conference/) puts the founding meeting in the week of 24 May 1976 in Columbia, Missouri, with 45 delegates. The motto is Latin for accuracy, power, speed (rule 1.1.3 of the handgun book and https://www.ipsc.org/what-is-ipsc/).
- Organisation: constitution, January 2025 edition (https://ipsc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/IPSC-Constitution-January-2025-Edition-Final-31-Dec-2024.pdf, SHA-256 `98d2d2de89b38943a14f1d2c5116006670b93a5e6396ab72f6a54b1086594be6`). IPSC takes no individual members. Bodies affiliate as a Region, which must be democratically run and represent the practical shooters of that Region. A Region may not sanction a match inside another Region's borders without that Region's approval (handgun rule 6.1.7).
- Regions: https://ipsc.org/regions/ lists 111 entries in four zones (African, Australasian, European, Pan-American), for example Germany (GER), United States (USA), Great Britain (GBR), with one provisional Region (Oman). The page gives a postal address and a contact per region, not a rule book.

### What the handgun rule book defines (January 2026)

- **Disciplines and divisions.** Centerfire handgun, rifle, shotgun, PCC; rimfire handgun and mini rifle; Action Air. Handgun divisions: Open, Standard, Classic, Production, Production Optics, Revolver, Optics (appendices D1 to D6). Each match recognizes at least one division, each scored separately (rule 6.2.1). A competitor declares one division per match.
- **Categories** (appendix A2): Lady, junior bands (under 14, 18, 21), senior bands (over 50, 60, 70) and lady variants. Division recognition needs a minimum number of competitors that depends on match level.
- **Match levels** (appendix A1): levels I to V, with recommended minimum rounds (40, 80, 150, 300, 450) and stages for handgun.
- **Course of fire types** (chapter 1). Short course: at most 12 rounds. Medium: at most 24. Long: at most 32. At most 9 scoring hits from one location or view. The approved balance is 3 short to 2 medium to 1 long. Classifiers and shoot-offs (at most 12 rounds per array, mandatory reload) are special courses.
- **What is fixed vs. left free.** The book calls IPSC matches "freestyle" (rule 1.1.5): after the start signal a course must not require a mandatory reload or dictate stance, position or location, and handgun and shotgun targets are shot "as and when visible". Exceptions: short courses and classifiers may require reloads and positions, and general courses may require strong hand or weak hand only. Not fixed: distance, round count inside the caps, target order, start position (set per stage in the written stage briefing). Fixed: the target types, scoring values, minimum power factor, division equipment limits, and the 7 m minimum distance to steel (rule 2.1.3).
- **Targets** (appendices B and C). Two paper targets: the IPSC Target and the smaller IPSC Mini Target, both with zones A, C, D and a 0.5 cm (mini: 0.3 cm) non-scoring border. Scoring: A 5 points; C 4 (Major) or 3 (Minor); D 2 (Major) or 1 (Minor). Poppers and metal plates score 5 points. A miss or no-shoot hit costs 10 points. Diagram of the IPSC Target (published by IPSC, retrieved 2026-10-27): https://legacy.ipscadmin.org/diagrams/IPSC_ipsctarget_1.jpg , overall 45 cm by 57 cm. Mini Target and popper diagrams are on https://www.ipsc.org/ipsc-rules/diagrams/ . I did not read the zone dimensions off the mini target.
- **Hit factor** (rule 9.2.1): the only handgun method is "Comstock": unlimited time, unlimited shots, a stated number of best hits per target counts, the stage score is points minus penalties divided by time to two decimals, and the best hit factor on the stage gets the stage maximum with others scaled down. Stage and match totals are kept to 4 decimals.
- **Power factor** (section 5.6, appendix D): Minor and Major ratings. Minor floor 125. Major floor: 160 Open, 170 Standard, Classic and Revolver. Production, Production Optics and Optics have no Major. Revolvers with 7 or more rounds do not qualify for Major. The factor is tested with 3 rounds over a chronograph and is calculated from bullet weight and the mean velocity.
- **Classification:** the IPSC site describes an international classification system (ICS) (https://www.ipsc.org/what-is-ipsc/); I did not read its rules.

### What a record would have to hold (IPSC handgun)

Stage: round limit and course type, scoring method, number of targets by type, maximum points. Competitor result: division, category, power factor (Major or Minor), time, counts of A, C, D, miss, no-shoot and procedural penalties, hit factor, stage points. Match: level, number of stages. Distance and shot count per discipline are not rule-book values.

### Action Air

- Is a discipline of IPSC with its own book: Action Air Handgun Competition Rules, January 2026, linked from the rule books page under "Action Air Handgun" (72 pages, SHA-256 `f143252df02eae8db4c59d5a607431813d41d4e888fd94bac5b000e9bcdab415`), listed on the same rule books page and in the site menu under "Action Air Disciplines" (handgun, rifle, PCC). IPSC's overview says it recognizes four disciplines: handgun, rifle, shotgun and Action Air.
- Equipment: 6 mm projectiles, a magazine unit that holds both projectiles and propellant gas, blowback action for self-loading pistols, maximum power 2 joules or the legal limit of the host region if lower (rules 5.1.2 and 5.6.1). Its divisions: Open, Standard, Classic, Production, Production Optics.
- Same stage caps (12, 24, 32 projectiles). Scoring zones A 5, C 3, D 1, with no Major or Minor (appendix B2). Minimum distance to rigid targets is 2 m, not 7 m.
- The books I read do not mention "micro-regions". See the claims table.

## USPSA (United States Practical Shooting Association)

- Rules, edition 2026-09, effective 2026-09-01: https://rules.uspsa.org/uspsa (the site's own root page, https://rules.uspsa.org/ , lists the USPSA, Multigun and Steel Challenge books and says they are the official rule books). I read chapters 1, 4, 5, 9, 12 and appendices A1, B1, D1, D2, D4, D6, D7 online. The USPSA main site (https://uspsa.org/) refused automated access, so statements about its organisation below are **partial**.
- Relationship to IPSC: partial. A search excerpt of https://uspsa.org/pages/history says USPSA was incorporated as the US Region of IPSC (the excerpt says 1984; a USPSA media kit and a president's announcement excerpt say 1983, so the year is unverified). https://uspsa.org/pages/about is reported to say membership in USPSA includes IPSC membership. IPSC's regions page lists "IPSC United States - USA" (https://ipsc.org/regions/). USPSA's rule book calls the IPSC Target an approved target.
- Own rule book: confirmed. Differences from IPSC that matter for records: Short course 1 to 12 rounds, medium 13 to 20, long 21 and more (at most 32 at level III and higher), at most 8 scoring hits from one location (rules 1.2.1.1 to 1.2.1.3). Standard exercises, speed shoots (at most 16 rounds) and classifiers are special courses. Three scoring methods: Comstock, Virginia Count (limited shots, hit factor) and Fixed Time (limited time and shots, no hit factor, ranking by net points) (rule 9.2).
- Divisions (appendix D): Open, Limited, Limited-10, Production, Single-Stack, Revolver, Carry Optics, Pistol Caliber Carbine, Limited Optics. Power factor: Minor 125, Major 165 in Open, Limited and Revolver, no Major in Production and Carry Optics (appendices D1, D2, D4, D6, D7).
- Targets (appendix B1, rule 4.2.1): two cardboard types, "USPSA Target" and "IPSC Target", never mixed in one stage. Half-size versions of both exist. Zones for all cardboard: A 5; C 4 (Major) or 3 (Minor); D 2 or 1. The B zone of the USPSA target scores as C (phased out per a 2018 board directive). A miss or no-shoot hit costs twice the maximum value of the target.
- **Metric vs Classic:** neither rule book names a target "Metric" or "Classic" (searched all read pages). The official names are "USPSA Target" and "IPSC Target". The IPSC Target diagram shows an octagon-like outline with no separate head box. The USPSA Target image could not be retrieved, so its shape is unverified here.
- Licence: footer "(c) 2026 United States Practical Shooting Association. All rights reserved. Reproduced under authority of USPSA, National Range Officers Institute". No reuse licence.

- Rule documents hub: https://www.idpa.com/idpa-match-rules/ . On 2026-10-27 the site returned a "temporarily offline for security maintenance" page (HTTP 503) for the home page and for the rulebook PDF, so I could not read the rule book. Everything in this section is **partial**, taken from search-engine excerpts of idpa.com pages, and should be re-read from the PDF before use.
- Current book per excerpts: 2026.2 Rulebook, https://www.idpa.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-IDPA-Rulebook-2.pdf , amended 10 January 2026, in force 15 January 2026 (https://www.idpa.com/2026-idpa-rule-documents-update/). Companion documents: 2026 Equipment Appendices and 2026 Match Administration Rules. Free to read. The Match Administration Rules PDF carries "all rights reserved"; no reuse terms found.
- Divisions per excerpt: SSP, ESP, CDP, CCP, CO, REV, BUG, PCC (eight), the last three optional. Magazine limits per division from the excerpt: SSP 15, ESP, CO and CCP 10, CDP and BUG semi-auto 8, REV 6, BUG revolver 5, PCC 30.
- Scoring per excerpts of the rulebook and a December 2025 club briefing on idpa.com: time plus seconds. Hits in the -1 or -3 zone add 1 or 3 s; a complete miss adds 5 s; poppers count as -0 if they fall and -5 if not; non-threat hits cost 5 s each. This is time plus points down, not hit factor and not rings.
- Distances per excerpts (conflicting readings, unverified): a limit of 20 yards for targets in scenario stages and 30 yards in standards stages, and a rule that 75 percent of required shots be taken at 15 yards or closer; also a rule against requiring shots beyond 10 yards (scenario) or 15 yards (standards) at head-sized targets or smaller. Match administration rules set minimum round counts by tier (100, 125, 150) with a 12-stage minimum at the highest.
- Concealed-carry gear, cover and a fixed order of engagement: **unverified**. The excerpts only showed a club stage that asks for near-to-far engagement of each array.

## Steel Challenge (SCSA)

- Rules, edition 2026-09, effective 2026-09-01, published by USPSA at https://rules.uspsa.org/scsa . The SCSA site (https://scsa.org/) refused automated access, so its history and classification pages are **partial** (search excerpts).
- Format (confirmed, chapters 2 and 9): each stage has five plates, four standard plates and a stop plate. Each standard plate should be hit before the stop plate. A competitor may fire as many rounds as needed. A stage has five timed strings and the slowest is dropped, so the best four count (Outer Limits: best three of four). Maximum time per string 30 s. A missed standard plate adds 3 s. The match score is the sum of stage times, lowest wins. So scoring is time, with penalty seconds, not time only.
- Layout (confirmed): all stages are fired from one 3 ft by 3 ft box, except Outer Limits (three 4 ft by 4 ft boxes) and Showdown (two 3 ft boxes). Round plates are 5 ft high, rectangles 5 ft 6 in. Eight official stage diagrams (appendix B: 5 to Go, Showdown, Smoke and Hope, Outer Limits, Accelerator, The Pendulum, Speed Option, Roundabout). Plate distances are in the diagram images, which I did not read. Rule 2.2 names 20 and 35 yard plates for Outer Limits (confirmed). The other stages' plate distances are **unverified** here, but fixed per stage.
- Divisions: 11 (appendix D: Open, Limited, Rimfire Pistol, Production, Single Stack, Revolver, Carry Optics, Pistol Caliber Carbine, Rimfire Rifle, Limited Optics, Rimfire Revolver).
- Relationship to USPSA: the rule book is published and copyrighted by USPSA, and a search excerpt shows scsa.org calls itself "an organization of USPSA". Founding year and 2005 integration (from the search I made): unverified. SCSA's own history page was reported as "under development".

## ICORE (International Confederation of Revolver Enthusiasts)

- Site https://www.icore.org/ , rule book revision 2026-01-28, 19 pages, https://www.icore.org/documents/rulebooks/ICORE_Rule_Book_2026-01-28.pdf (SHA-256 `dcfba8c697d814193514c92fb537b351bec3470f26147602f51822d2209497bb`). Free to read. Footer: "1999 International Confederation of Revolver Enthusiasts. All rights reserved."
- About page (https://www.icore.org/about.php): founded in 1991 by Mike and Sharon Higashi, incorporated as a nonprofit in Arizona on 24 September 1999, exclusively for revolvers. It describes itself as combining elements of the Bianchi Cup, IPSC and Steel Challenge. International regions are mentioned, the list is not on the loaded page (unverified).
- Divisions (rule 12.1): Classic, Limited, Limited 6, Open, Big 6. Rimfire is a separate match format, also revolver only. Each division is shot and scored separately.
- Targets (rules 7.1, appendix 1): the ICORE or NRA D-1 and AP-1 targets, plus falling and standing steel. Scoring by total time: zone A adds 0 s, B adds 1 s (0 s in Big 6), C adds 2 s. Penalties are 5 s for a miss, a no-shoot hit, a premature start, a foot fault, a failure to engage and a procedural. A stop plate miss costs 30 s.
- Stage design (chapter 4): the written stage briefing sets scoring type (shots limited, shots unlimited, or par time), reloads, order and positions. Distances are set per stage. Classifier stages are published on the site with fixed dimensions.

## 3-Gun and multigun

- The primary rule book found is USPSA's Rifle, Shotgun and Multigun Rules, edition 2026-09: https://rules.uspsa.org/multigun . A multigun match must use at least two firearm types and should use two or more in each course. A match may also be rifle only or shotgun only. The book is not usable for a handgun-only or PCC-only match.
- So the owner's wording "rifle, shotgun and handgun in one course" is a common form, not a rule: the rule asks for at least two types per match.
- Course types: general courses, standard exercises (at most 32 rounds per string for rifle or shotgun, 48 for multigun), speed shoots. Scoring methods: Comstock hit factor, limited-time Comstock (rifle targets at least 100 yards away) and fixed time, plus a separate time-plus method (section 9.3: a target needs one A hit or two hits inside the perforations or it costs 5 or 10 s). Six divisions: Open, Tactical, Limited, Heavy Metal Tactical, Heavy Metal Limited, Modified. Distances are set per stage.
- The other multigun bodies named in the issue (for example 3-Gun Nation): **unverified**. The domain 3gunnation.com returned unrelated gambling content when loaded, so I did not use it. IPSC has no multigun discipline in the books I read. Its nearest forms are the Tournament (one match whose stages are assigned to different firearm types, handgun rule 6.1.4) and the Grand Tournament (separate firearm-specific matches combined, rule 6.1.5).

## Open points for the owner's decision ticket

- Whether the Federations list covers bodies (IPSC, USPSA, IDPA, SCSA, ICORE) or countries (IPSC regions). The sources support both, but only the body has a rule book.
- Whether a stage-based or hit-factor discipline belongs in the `discipline` record at all. The record requires one distance and one face. The honest values here are "no fixed distance" (IPSC handgun, USPSA, IDPA, ICORE) and "set per stage" (Steel Challenge, multigun).
- Whether to hold IPSC, USPSA and Steel Challenge target shapes, given the IPSC trademark on "IPSC Targets".
- Re-read IDPA from its PDF once idpa.com is back, and read the Steel Challenge stage diagram images for plate distances.
